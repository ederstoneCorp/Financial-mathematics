# SMA 2207 scope filter

## Status

This is the working scope used to build the learning platform. It is informed by the SMA 2207 topics previously discussed and by publicly available JKUAT past-paper material. It is **not represented as a verbatim or officially approved departmental syllabus**. The 800-page source PDF is an ActEd CT1 Combined Materials Pack (2013), not a JKUAT course outline.

## Core learning pathway

| Platform module | Required learning outcomes represented in the platform | Relevant CT1 source |
|---|---|---|
| Cashflow models | Identify receipts and payments, sign conventions, timing, and cashflow timelines | Chapter 1 |
| Simple and compound interest | Accumulate and discount single sums; solve for principal, rate, time and accumulated amount | Chapter 2 |
| Interest and discount rates | Convert nominal and effective rates; compare rates convertible at different frequencies; relate interest and discount | Chapter 3 |
| Force of interest | Use constant and time-varying force of interest and derive accumulation factors | Chapter 3 |
| Real and money rates | Relate nominal money growth, inflation and real growth | Chapter 4; retain as a supporting topic pending lecturer confirmation |
| Discounting and accumulation | Value equal/unequal cashflows at a focal date; handle varying rates and continuous cashflows | Chapter 5 |
| Annuities | Derive and use present/accumulated values for immediate, due, deferred, periodic and continuous payment streams | Chapters 6–7 |
| Equations of value | Equate cashflows at a common valuation date and solve for unknown amounts or rates | Chapter 8 |
| Loan repayment | Calculate level instalments, interest/capital portions, and outstanding balances, including changed rates or payment frequencies | Chapter 9 |
| Sinking funds | Calculate regular deposits required to accumulate a target fund | Chapters 6–7 |
| Continuous cashflows | Accumulate or discount continuous payment streams under constant or varying force of interest | Chapters 3 and 5–6 |
| Short-term investment returns | Calculate holding-period, simple annual and effective annual returns from purchase/redemption values | Use only the relevant elementary return methods; do not import the whole CT1 securities syllabus |

## Past-paper cross-check

The publicly indexed SMA 2207 papers located during development provide examples of the following assessed question styles:

- 2022/2023 paper: time required for a sum to accumulate under simple and compound interest; converting a nominal rate convertible monthly to an annual effective discount rate.
- 2023 paper: equivalent rate expressions and equations of value involving cashflows at different dates; loan repayment calculations.
- 2025/2026 supplementary paper: Treasury-bill holding period and simple/effective annual return; derivation of the future value of an ordinary annuity; semi-annual loan instalments; outstanding balance where the interest rate changes; accumulation of a continuous payment stream under a time-dependent force of interest; valuation of dated payments under a force of interest; ordering convertible interest and discount rates.

These papers are useful evidence of question styles and topic coverage, but they are not a substitute for the current lecturer's official teaching outline.

## Keep supplementary or out of the core

- Full fixed-interest bond pricing, redemption-yield analysis and tax treatment.
- Project appraisal methods such as IRR and payback, unless the lecturer confirms them.
- Depreciation models.
- Arbitrage pricing and forward contracts.
- Term-structure models, duration, convexity and immunisation.
- Stochastic interest-rate models.
- Advanced derivatives and other CT1/actuarial material not supported by the SMA 2207 course coverage.

## Source-use rules

1. Use the CT1 pack to support explanations and mathematical derivations only where they map to the working course scope.
2. Write original explanations, examples and practice questions. Do not reproduce the proprietary ActEd notes or question bank.
3. Use Kenyan shilling examples where they clarify the mathematics, without changing the mathematical assumptions.
4. Keep every calculator explicit about rate basis, compounding frequency, payment timing, number of periods and valuation date.
5. If the lecturer's official outline is later supplied, reconcile this map against it and update the platform's core/supplementary labels.
